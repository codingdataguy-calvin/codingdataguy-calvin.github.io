Section 01

Executive Summary

From June to October 2026 I was the Data & AI Governance Lead on a Decision Inc UK engagement with a global renewable energy developer. The business had grown largely through acquisition, had no formal data governance function, and was rolling out AI assistants to thousands of staff on top of a data platform that was itself mid-migration.

My brief was to build governance that let the business trust its data and adopt AI with the right controls in place. I ran it as one engagement with two workstreams sitting on a shared foundation:

  • Workstream A: Data governance and the Purview platform. Implemented Microsoft Purview as the enterprise governance platform across 4 data sources, stood up a Finance data product with live data quality scoring, and authored a federated data ownership framework covering 12 business domains.
  • Workstream B: AI governance foundations and the MCP gateway. Proposed and oversaw an MCP server gateway that now sits between staff and Claude, Copilot and ChatGPT. It reprofiled 8,000+ users into role-based access tiers and, using Purview DLP, stopped 100+ Highly Confidential assets from being processed by AI tools.

Section 02

Context & Starting Point

The client is a renewable energy developer and operator working across several continents. Years of acquisitions had left data spread across platforms and regions, with nobody formally accountable for most of it.

  • No governance function. There was no agreed framework, policy set, ownership model or steward network to build on.
  • A platform in transition. Analytics ran on a legacy Azure Synapse estate while a new Microsoft Fabric platform was being built alongside it. Governance had to work on both.
  • AI arriving faster than controls. AI assistants were being adopted across the business, and they inherited default permissions that had often been granted at company level. In practice, an AI tool could see whatever a user's tenant permissions allowed, whether or not that was appropriate.
  • Regulatory pressure. Parts of the business fall within the scope of the EU AI Act, so AI use needed an inventory, risk classification and a policy before it scaled further.
Why this mattered

Data governance and AI governance aren't separate problems here. An AI tool is only as safe as the classification, ownership and access rules underneath it, so the AI controls had to be built on the same foundation as the data platform.

Section 03

Foundation: Assessment & Operating Model

In the first month I completed a governance and security assessment of the legacy data platform. It surfaced 11 core risk exposures and control gaps, and I wrote a remediation plan for each one. Those plans became a prioritised roadmap that senior stakeholders adopted as the basis for the engagement.

Alongside the assessment, I secured executive sponsorship and stakeholder sign-off on the governance framework, policies, standards and stewardship roles. That gave the business an agreed operating model before any tooling went in, which mattered: it's much easier to configure a platform when people have already agreed who owns what.

Section 04

Microsoft Purview Build & Rollout

I implemented Microsoft Purview as the enterprise governance platform and led its rollout across 4 data sources: Azure Synapse, Microsoft Fabric, Azure Blob Storage and SharePoint. I configured the Data Map, Unified Catalog, classification and business glossary, and switched on Data Quality, creating a single source of governed metadata for the analytics estate.

Rather than try to catalogue everything at once, I scoped the first release around one high-value data product: the Finance cost centre report, used at CFO level. I ingested Finance data from all four sources, but the cost centre report stayed the primary data product, so the business could see governance working end to end on something it genuinely cared about. HR was set up as a placeholder domain, ready for the next phase.

Governance by design

Working with the analytics team, I extended these controls into the data platforms, warehouses and pipelines themselves, so governance is built in as new assets are created rather than bolted on afterwards. The same collection structure was mirrored on the new Fabric platform, so moving off the legacy estate doesn't mean starting governance again.

Section 05

Federated Data Ownership

An organisation built through acquisition doesn't have one way of using data, so a purely central ownership model wouldn't have stuck. I authored an enterprise-wide, federated data ownership framework, working with the business to map how data is really used before agreeing who should own it.

The result is 12 business domains with agreed owners. Every region shares the same domain structure, which keeps reporting consistent globally, while regional stewards handle the day-to-day work locally.

Section 06

The MCP Server Gateway

The core risk was simple. Claude, Copilot and ChatGPT were reaching company data through permissions that had been granted at company level, long before anyone was thinking about AI. Tightening each tool separately would have been slow and inconsistent.

I proposed a different approach: put a single Model Context Protocol (MCP) server gateway between staff and every AI tool, and govern access there. The idea was mine; Decision Inc engineers built it for the client, and I acted as design authority and supervised the build through testing and go-live.

Three access tiers for 8,000+ users

Every user was reprofiled by how critical AI is to their work, then given role-based access that controls which tools they use, for what purpose and for how long.

Tier 1
Technical
Engineers and data specialists who build with AI and need the broadest, most capable access.
Tier 2
Curators
Business owners accountable for data in their domain, with access matched to the data they own.
Tier 3
Consumers
Every other employee, with governed, purpose-limited access to approved AI tools.

Section 07

EU AI Act Controls

I turned the EU AI Act into working controls for the parts of the business it affects. That started with an AI use-case inventory covering 5 AI use cases, each classified against the Act's four risk tiers, and finished with an AI usage policy aligned to ISO/IEC 42001.

Section 08

Adoption & Enablement

Governance only works if people use it. I drove adoption through data steward onboarding, regional training and executive engagement, and tracked progress against defined governance KPIs.

Section 10

What I'd Take Forward

  • Prove the platform on one thing people care about. Narrowing the first release to a single CFO-level data product got governance working end to end, instead of half-configured everywhere.
  • Govern AI at the access layer, not tool by tool. One gateway gave consistent rules across three different AI products and made it possible to add tools later without redoing the controls.
  • Agree ownership before configuring tooling. The signed-off ownership model made the Purview collections, catalogue domains and AI access tiers straightforward to design, because the hard conversations had already happened.
